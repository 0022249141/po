The median occupancy-event count is about `20.47%` higher in New York than London. In contrast, median mean-bin-occupancy differs by less than `1%`, so the aggregate distinction is more evident in session span / total occupancy-event count than in median occupancy density per occupied bin.

## Distribution caution

The aggregate distributions are not symmetric. London has a maximum recorded range of `41,429` ticks versus `27,183` for New York, while its median remains lower. London also has a maximum occupancy-event count of `314,390` versus `261,821` for New York. Therefore the result should not be reduced to a statement that one session is uniformly more volatile on every day.

The frozen study deliberately performs no statistical significance test. It also does not control here for month, volatility regime, macro-event day, weekday, or other conditioning variables. Those are separate robustness questions and must not be retrofitted into this result.

## Tokyo boundary

Tokyo has only `12` complete sessions in the canonical feed under the fixed 09:00–18:00 Asia/Tokyo research window. Its median range is `8,443` ticks and median occupancy events are `76,039.5`, but the frozen spec classifies Tokyo as secondary descriptive context because the complete-session sample is materially smaller than London/New York.

No cross-session ranking or trading inference should be made from the Tokyo figures until a separately frozen study addresses the sample imbalance.

## Interpretation

**Observed data:** on this canonical dataset and frozen complete-only policy, New York has a higher aggregate median range and higher aggregate median occupancy-event count than London, while median mean-bin-occupancy is nearly the same.

**Not established:** statistical significance, persistence through time, causal explanation, predictive value, trading edge, profitability, POC, Value Area, ICT kill-zone equivalence, centralized volume, order flow, dealer inventory, or bank positioning.
